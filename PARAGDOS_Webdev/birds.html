Cat Care Guide

Learn about cat breeds, what they eat, their health weaknesses, and how to keep them happy and healthy.

Introduction

Cats are domesticated animals known for their independence, agility, and strong hunting instincts. Although cats can be independent, they still require proper nutrition, clean water, a safe environment, enrichment, grooming, and regular veterinary care.

🐱 Popular Cat Breeds

Domestic Shorthair / Local Cat

Traits: Mixed breed, short coat, very common in the Philippines, hardy, adaptable, low grooming needs.

Origin: Worldwide — the most common pet cat type in Filipino homes.

Persian

Traits: Long thick coat, flat face, calm and quiet temperament; prefers a peaceful home.

Origin: Iran (Persia)

Maine Coon

Traits: Large size, bushy tail, shaggy coat; friendly, intelligent, often called "gentle giants".

Origin: United States (New England)

Siamese

Traits: Sleek body, blue almond eyes, distinctive dark points on ears, face, paws; very vocal, social, and intelligent.

Origin: Thailand (formerly Siam)

British Shorthair

Traits: Round face, dense plush coat, sturdy build; calm, easy-going, adapts well to apartment life.

Origin: United Kingdom

Ragdoll

Traits: Large, bright blue eyes, soft color-point coat; very gentle, relaxes completely when held.

Origin: United States

🍖 What Cats Eat

🐱 Cats are obligate carnivores — they must eat meat to survive and cannot be healthy on a vegetarian diet.

✅ Primary diet: High-quality commercial cat food (wet or dry) formulated to meet their nutritional needs.

✅ Key nutrients: Need taurine, protein from animal sources, calcium, and vitamins A & D — these cannot be replaced by plant food.

✅ Safe treats: Cooked chicken or fish (no bones), small amounts of egg — always plain, no salt or seasoning.

💧 Water: Provide fresh clean water daily — cats often prefer running water; wet food helps keep them hydrated.

❌ Never feed: Onions, garlic, chocolate, grapes, raisins, milk (most adult cats are lactose intolerant), raw fish/meat regularly, or dog food long-term.

⚠️ Cat Weaknesses & Health Vulnerabilities

Hidden illness: Cats naturally hide pain and sickness — by the time you notice, they may already be very sick.

Urinary tract issues: Very common — especially in males; can block and become life-threatening quickly.

Taurine deficiency: Causes heart and eye damage — only prevented by proper meat-based nutrition.

Heat sensitivity: Cats overheat easily — cannot cool down as well as humans; never leave in enclosed cars or direct sun without shade.

Poison sensitivity: More easily poisoned than many animals — lilies are especially deadly; also harmed by essential oils, some houseplants, and common human medicines.

Dental disease: By age 3, most cats have tartar, gum infection, or tooth decay — causes pain and organ damage if ignored.

Parasites: Fleas, ticks, worms, and mites are common — even indoor cats can get them from shoes or other pets.

Obesity: Overfeeding + low activity = extra weight that stresses joints, heart, and shortens lifespan.

🚨 Early Warning Signs

Not eating or drinking normally for more than 12–24 hours

Repeated vomiting or loose stools

Straining to urinate, crying in litter box, or producing no urine — seek vet help immediately

Hiding more than usual or avoiding interaction

Sudden weight loss or gain

Changes in grooming — over-grooming or stopping grooming entirely

Sleeping constantly, reluctance to jump, or obvious pain

🛡️ How to Keep Cats Healthy

Provide balanced, age-appropriate food and constant fresh water.

Keep litter boxes clean — one per cat plus one extra is ideal.

Give them scratching posts, places to climb, and safe hiding spots.

Play with them daily — toys, chasing, hunting-style games keep them fit and happy.

Keep cats indoors or in fully enclosed outdoor spaces — protects them from traffic, fights, disease, and poison.

Keep all dangerous plants, chemicals, and human food out of reach.

Visit the vet annually (twice a year for seniors) for checkups, vaccines, and parasite prevention.

Introduction

Birds are intelligent, social, feathered creatures with strong flocking instincts. They need proper nutrition, safe housing, companionship, stimulation, grooming, and regular avian vet care to thrive.

🐦 Popular Pet Bird Species

Budgerigar (Budgie / Parakeet)

Traits: Small, playful, colorful; great talkers; very common in Filipino homes

Origin: Australia

Cockatiel

Traits: Medium-sized, charming crest, sweet whistler; gentle and affectionate

Origin: Australia

Lovebird

Traits: Small, vibrant, fiercely loyal; energetic and intelligent — very popular in the Philippines

Origin: Africa

Canary

Traits: Small, beautiful songster; more independent, lovely to watch and hear

Origin: Canary Islands

African Grey Parrot

Traits: Exceptional intelligence & speaking ability; sensitive — needs lots of mental engagement

Origin: Central & West Africa

🍽️ What Birds Eat

Most pet birds are omnivores — a seed-only diet is NOT healthy long-term!


✅ Recommended:

  • High-quality species-appropriate pellets as the main diet
  • Fresh dark leafy greens, carrots, bell peppers, broccoli, squash
  • Small portions of papaya, banana, melon, or berries
  • Sprouted seeds, cooked egg (great protein), cooked beans
  • Fresh clean water — change daily

❌ NEVER Feed:

  • Avocado, chocolate, caffeine, onions, garlic
  • Fruit seeds/pits (apple, cherry), rhubarb
  • Salty, sugary, fried, or highly processed food
  • Dairy products

⚠️ Health Vulnerabilities

  • Hidden illness: Birds mask sickness — by the time you notice, they may be critical
  • Respiratory sensitivity: Harmed by smoke, Teflon fumes, scented candles, aerosols
  • Vitamin A deficiency: Common on seed diets → weak immunity & infections
  • Egg-binding: Life-threatening in females — caused by low calcium or stress
  • Feather plucking: Caused by boredom, poor diet, or parasites
  • Fatty liver disease: Too many seeds + no exercise = shortened lifespan
  • Night fright: Sudden scares in dark cause injury — cover cage at night

🚨 Early Warning Signs — See an Avian Vet Quickly

  • Fluffed up, sleeping more than usual, quiet for 24+ hours
  • Not eating or drinking normally
  • Runny/discolored droppings lasting more than a day
  • Discharge from eyes/nostrils; wheezing, fast breathing, tail bobbing
  • Sitting at cage bottom, weak, unsteady
  • Feather plucking, bare patches, bleeding
  • Sudden changes in voice or behavior

🛡️ How to Keep Birds Healthy & Happy

  • Feed pellets + fresh produce; limit seeds to treats
  • Spacious cage — wide enough to stretch both wings fully
  • Natural perches of different thicknesses; keep everything clean
  • Enrichment: swings, bells, foraging & chewable toys — rotate often
  • Interact daily; single birds need your attention; pairs need each other
  • Place away from drafts, direct sun, and kitchen fumes
  • Cover cage at night for quiet, undisturbed sleep
  • Annual checkups with an avian veterinarian
